# LinkedIn outreach playbook (2026-11-14)

How to find and write to the people who can move a job you applied to (recruiters, hiring
managers, founders, possible referrers), especially in Germany, and how to tell what works.
Measured by **meaningful replies, confirmed referrals and interviews per person targeted**.
Nothing in here sends anything: every message is still yours to send.

## The short version

- **The invite works; what comes after it was missing.** 25% of invites were accepted, which is
  normal in sales data. But 8 of the 10 people who accepted in week one never heard from you
  again, and on Nov 14 those same 8 still showed no reply and no follow-up on record (the app has
  no box for it, so check LinkedIn before sending anything).
- **The app can't see week two.** 26 notes on jobs you applied to since Nov 9 have no "sent" tick.
  Ticking, or a 20-minute look at LinkedIn, comes before any test.
- **Try ending each note with a question someone can answer in five seconds.** The open ending
  ("I'd love to hear what your team is building next...") ended 36 of the notes written; the data
  can't tell whether any note was answered directly. In email data, messages with a question get
  more replies. That's a reason to test it, not proof.
- **A referral from someone who knows you is worth more than any message**, partly because
  people refer those who already fit. Strangers usually can't refer you, especially after you've
  applied. The realistic ask is "could you flag my application?".
- **Germany is the easiest of the four visa countries compared** (no sponsor licence, no degree
  check on the IT route, the lowest pay floor; Finland, Spain and Italy weren't checked). But most
  German postings either want German or say nothing about visas. The plan: say what the employer
  would have to do, and ask first where the posting is silent.

## What this is based on

- Your own outreach: the week-one review (Nov 7), the old tracker, and the app's database, read
  only. Week one is small: 64 invites, 10 accepted, 2 replies by the review's count (the app adds
  2 accepts and 1 reply). Nothing in it is proven.
- 35 live job postings read on Nov 14 (20 in the UK, Netherlands, Ireland, UAE and remote; 15 in
  Germany), to test where contact names actually appear.
- The law text and official pages for Germany, the UK, the Netherlands and Ireland.
- Outside data on messages and referrals. Almost none of it measures job seekers writing to
  companies: most is sales outreach or recruiters writing to candidates. It's labelled that way
  everywhere, and treated as a reason to test, never as a promise.
